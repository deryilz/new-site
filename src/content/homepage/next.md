Right now, the computer-related field I'm most interested in is programming language design, specifically how type systems work. I think there's still an unexplored future for systems capable of evaluating code at compile-time (like [Zig's `comptime`](https://zig.guide/language-basics/comptime/), but more general) or systems that can typecheck efficiently at either phase (runtime or compile-time) without relying on macros.

I'm also very interested how proof systems can help us code, which is why I've been learning [Idris2](https://idris-lang.org/Idris2/) over the last couple months. I think proof-based software verification for open-source projects will become a big deal in the future, as they'll have a chance of finding the last few bugs once LLMs start to plateau. For the moment, though, it's definitely eye-opening [just how many bugs AI is finding](https://issues.chromium.org/issues?q=type:vulnerability%20status:fixed%20%22experimental%20security%20project%22&s=modified_time:desc) in large open-source codebases like Chromium.

Other than that, I'm just messing around with algorithmic puzzles and trying to get better at that sort of thing.
